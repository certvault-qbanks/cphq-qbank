      Performance and Process Improvement is the largest CPHQ category at 27 scored items. The content outline names three methods (Lean, PDSA and Six Sigma) and three tools (fishbone diagrams, FMEA and process maps). The exam rarely asks you to define them. It asks you to choose one.

      The Model for Improvement and PDSA

      The Model for Improvement, popularized by IHI, starts with three questions and then tests changes in rapid Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles.

      1. What are we trying to accomplish?

        A specific, time-bound aim: "Reduce ED door-to-provider time to under 30 minutes by June."

      2. How will we know a change is an improvement?

        Outcome, process and balancing measures tracked over time.

      3. What change can we make that will result in improvement?

        Ideas from evidence, other organizations and frontline staff, tested one at a time.

      Exam pattern

      When a scenario says "test," "pilot," "small scale" or "learn whether," PDSA is usually the answer. Multiple small cycles are better than one big one.

      Lean: remove waste, improve flow

      Lean, rooted in the Toyota Production System, defines value from the patient's perspective and removes everything else. Tools include value stream maps, 5S, standard work, visual management and kanban. The eight wastes are easy to recall as DOWNTIME. Tap each letter for a healthcare example.

      Six Sigma: reduce variation and defects

      Six Sigma uses statistics to make a process more consistent. At "six sigma" performance, a process produces about 3.4 defects per million opportunities. Projects follow DMAIC:

      1. Define

        Problem, scope, customer requirements and project charter.

      2. Measure

        Baseline performance and a reliable data collection plan.

      3. Analyze

        Find and verify root causes with data.

      4. Improve

        Develop, test and implement solutions.

      5. Control

        Sustain gains with monitoring, control charts and standard work.

      Lean Six Sigma combines both: Lean for speed and flow, Six Sigma for precision.

      Teams and leading change

      The outline also covers establishing teams and leading change. Know these roles:

      RoleResponsibility
      Executive sponsorRemoves barriers, secures resources and connects the work to strategy
      Team leaderManages the project, agenda and timeline
      FacilitatorGuides process and tools, keeps discussion productive and neutral
      Process ownerAccountable for the process and for sustaining the change
      Frontline membersKnow how the work is really done; essential for buy-in

      Expect questions on resistance to change. The best answers involve engaging the people affected early, communicating the "why" with data, and celebrating small wins.

      Practice questionPerformance & Process Improvement

      Patients in an outpatient infusion center wait an average of 90 minutes from check-in to treatment. Much of the delay comes from walking between rooms, waiting for pharmacy and duplicate paperwork. Which method best fits?

      The problems described (walking, waiting and duplicate paperwork) are classic Lean wastes: motion, waiting and extra processing. A value stream map shows each step and where time is lost. Six Sigma targets variation and defects, while RCA and FMEA focus on safety events and risks.

      Practice questionPerformance & Process Improvement

      An improvement team has reduced catheter-associated urinary tract infections for three consecutive months. What should the team do to sustain the gain?

      Sustainability is the "Control" (or "Act") stage: standardize the new process, assign ownership, and keep monitoring, often with a control chart, so backsliding is caught early. Disbanding or stopping measurement risks losing the improvement.
