      About two in three US candidates pass the CPHQ. That's encouraging, but it also means roughly one in three walks out needing a retake. The good news: most failures trace back to a handful of predictable study mistakes.

      The published pass rate

      NAHQ's 2024 domestic candidate handbook reports a 65% pass rate for US candidates in 2023. Third-party sites often quote a 65–75% range; the handbook figure is the one to trust.

      International candidates have a separate handbook, and rates can differ by year and form.

      How the exam is scored

      • Scaled score of 200–800. Your raw score on the 125 scored items converts to this scale. 600 passes.
      • Not curved. A panel of experts set the cut score using the Angoff method, estimating how a minimally competent quality professional would perform. You pass on your own performance.
      • Equated across forms. If your version is slightly harder, the conversion adjusts so the standard stays fair.
      • Category scores are feedback only. You can't fail a single category. Only the overall score counts.

      Why this matters

      Because the exam isn't curved, there's no advantage to testing on a "slow" day or month. Test when your practice scores say you're ready.

      Six reasons candidates fall short

      1Studying by intuition instead of by item count

      Regulatory topics feel important, so candidates over-study them. But Regulatory and Accreditation is 8 of 125 items, while Process Improvement and Data Analytics together are 53. Try the exercise below to see how your instincts compare.

      2Memorizing definitions

      About 77% of items test application or analysis. Knowing what an FMEA is won't help if you can't tell when to use one instead of an RCA.

      3Under-preparing for statistics

      Health Data Analytics is 26 items and is widely considered the hardest category. Control chart interpretation and measure design need hands-on practice. Start with our data analytics guide.

      4Answering from their own hospital's habits

      The exam rewards best practice. If your organization skips steps, the exam answer probably doesn't.

      5Running out of time

      140 questions in 180 minutes is about 77 seconds each. Long scenario stems add up quickly if you haven't practiced a timed, full-length exam.

      6Not enough exam-style practice

      Reading builds familiarity; questions build judgment. First-time passers typically work through hundreds of scenario questions and review every explanation.

      What first-time passers do differently

      1. Start with a diagnostic

        They find their weak categories in week one instead of week seven.

      2. Weight study by item count

        They spend the most time where the most points are.

      3. Practice decisions, daily

        Short question sets after every study block, with every explanation reviewed.

      4. Rehearse the full exam

        At least one timed, 140-question practice test to build pacing and stamina.

      5. Schedule when scores are steady

        They book the exam once practice scores hold around 75% on fresh questions.

      Practice questionHealth Data Analytics

      A quality analyst notices that the monthly central line infection rate dropped sharply after a new dressing protocol began. Before reporting success to leadership, what should the analyst do?

      A drop could be real improvement, random variation or an artifact of a changed definition or data source. A control chart shows whether the change is a special cause signal, and checking the measure definition rules out a measurement change. One comparison hospital is not a valid benchmark, and you keep monitoring to confirm improvement is sustained.

      If you don't pass

      Use your score report's category percentages as a map, not a verdict. Rebuild your plan around the weakest heavy-weight categories first, since a small category can swing a lot on just a few questions. Check the current NAHQ candidate handbook for retest timing and fees before rescheduling.
