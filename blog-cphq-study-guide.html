      The CPHQ rewards people who can use quality methods, not just define them. The fastest way to pass is to study the way the exam is built: weight your time by NAHQ's published item counts, practice decisions instead of definitions, and rehearse under time pressure.

      What the CPHQ exam looks like

      The Certified Professional in Healthcare Quality exam is a computer-delivered, multiple-choice test offered year-round at PSI test centers or online with remote proctoring. Here is what you're walking into:

      ElementDetail
      Questions140 total: 125 scored, 15 unscored pretest items you cannot identify
      Time3 hours (about 77 seconds per question)
      ScoringScaled 200–800; 600 passes
      StandardCriterion-referenced (Angoff method), not curved
      Scheduling window90 days after payment
      EligibilityNo degree or experience gate; written for about 2 years of hands-on quality work

      What this means for you

      Flashcards cover roughly 29 of 125 scored items. For everything else, the question is "what should the quality professional do next?" Practice choosing a tool, reading a chart and prioritizing a step.

      The two dark bars add up to 53 items, or 42% of your score. Regulatory and Accreditation, which many people assume dominates a quality exam, is only 8 items. Open each category below for what to master.

      27Performance & Process Improvement

      Forming teams and defining scope, leading change, and running improvement projects. The outline names Lean, PDSA and Six Sigma as methods and fishbone diagrams, FMEA and process maps as tools.

      Master: choosing the right method for a scenario, team roles, evaluating whether a project succeeded. Deep dive: PDSA, Lean and Six Sigma.

      26Health Data Analytics

      Designing data collection, building measures (numerators, denominators, thresholds), sampling, dashboards, descriptive statistics, correlation, regression, t-tests and statistical process control.

      Master: common vs. special cause variation, which control chart fits which data, and measure types. Deep dive: Health Data Analytics made simple.

      19Quality Leadership & Integration

      Strategic planning, aligning quality with organizational goals, supporting the governing body, assessing safety culture and engaging stakeholders.

      Master: what the board is accountable for, how to prioritize improvement opportunities, and how to secure resources.

      18Patient Safety

      Safety culture, human factors, high reliability, teamwork, event reporting, sentinel event review, root cause analysis and proactive risk assessment.

      Master: RCA vs. FMEA, Just Culture responses, and strong vs. weak corrective actions. Deep dive: Patient Safety guide.

      16Quality Review & Accountability

      Compliance with guidelines and pathways, value-based payment programs, documentation, practitioner evaluation (OPPE and FPPE), patient experience and confidentiality of quality records.

      Master: when FPPE vs. OPPE applies, how quality ties to reimbursement, and protecting peer review information.

      11Population Health & Care Transitions

      Population health data, prevention and disease management, health equity, social determinants of health and care transitions.

      Master: readmission drivers and interventions (medication reconciliation, teach-back, timely follow-up) and using equity data to stratify measures.

      8Regulatory & Accreditation

      Choosing accreditation options, regulatory awareness, compliance monitoring and survey readiness.

      Master: the role of accreditors with CMS deeming authority and continuous readiness. Study it in proportion; it is the smallest category.

      An 8-week roadmap that works

      1. Week 1: Read the handbook and outline

        Download the current Candidate Handbook and Detailed Content Outline from nahq.org. You must attest to reading the handbook when you apply. Take a 50-question diagnostic to find your baseline.

      2. Weeks 2–3: Process improvement

        PDSA, Lean, Six Sigma, team roles, change management and the named tools. Do 20–30 practice questions after each study block.

      3. Weeks 3–4: Health data analytics

        Measure design, sampling, descriptive statistics and control charts. Work problems by hand; this category punishes passive reading.

      4. Week 5: Leadership, review and accountability

        Governance, strategic alignment, OPPE and FPPE, value-based programs and confidentiality.

      5. Week 6: Patient safety, population health, regulatory

        RCA, FMEA, Just Culture, high reliability, care transitions, equity and survey readiness.

      6. Week 7: Full-length timed exam

        140 questions in 3 hours. Review every explanation, including the ones you got right by guessing, and log your misses by category.

      7. Week 8: Target weak areas and rest

        Spend 70% of your time on your weakest heavy-weight categories. Take a second timed exam mid-week. Stop heavy studying the day before.

      Test-day strategy

      • Pace at 75 seconds per question. At question 70 you should have at least 90 minutes left.
      • Answer everything. The 15 pretest items are hidden, so no question is safe to skip.
      • Pick the best practice answer, not your hospital's answer. The exam tests the textbook-correct next step.
      • Watch for "first" and "best". Several options may be reasonable; one comes before the others (often: gather data or form the team before implementing).
      • Flag and move. Don't let one statistics item eat three minutes.

      Common trap

      Answers that jump straight to education or a new policy are often wrong when a stronger, system-level fix is available. Training is a weak corrective action.

      Practice questionPerformance & Process Improvement

      A quality manager is asked to reduce delays in the emergency department. Leadership wants a new triage protocol implemented next week. What should the quality manager do first?

      Changing a process before you understand it risks solving the wrong problem and leaves you no baseline to prove improvement. Mapping the current state and collecting baseline data comes first. Benchmarking and target-setting follow, then testing changes (ideally with small PDSA cycles) before spreading them.

      Frequently asked questions

      140 multiple-choice questions. 125 are scored and 15 are unscored pretest items mixed in throughout, so treat every question as if it counts. You have 3 hours.

      Most candidates plan 8 to 12 weeks at 5 to 8 hours a week. If you already work in quality and use control charts, PDSA and event review regularly, 6 to 8 weeks can be enough. Use the planner on this page to size it to your schedule.

      NAHQ's candidate handbook states there is no degree, license or experience gate. The exam is written for people who have performed the tasks on the content outline for about two years, so that is the difficulty level to prepare for.

      Start with Performance and Process Improvement (27 items) and Health Data Analytics (26 items). Together they are 42% of the scored exam.

      The exam is reported on a 200 to 800 scale, and 600 is the passing score.
